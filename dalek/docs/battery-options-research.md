# Battery options for the printed platform revision

Research date: 2026-09-12. Scope: retain the Bioenno battery and define two specific 12 V sealed lead-acid alternatives. This note supplies dimensions and electrical requirements for the new CAD. It does not certify a printed mount or loaded robot. The batteries, chargers and connectors have not been purchased or physically tested.

The outcome is one removable battery below one printed board platform. Changes to motor type, motor voltage, current limits and onboard charging are outside this work. The battery adapter, optional BOM entries and calculation cases are also integrated into the existing electrical generator and notes. Proof consists of primary manufacturer specifications, the existing circuit and firmware, the unchanged downstream connection endpoints, and explicit calculations below. Final mesh and print checks belong to the CAD revision.

## Supported battery models

Keep the existing Bioenno BLF-1206A: 12 V, 6 Ah, 72 Wh, 0.7 kg and 12 A continuous discharge. Its page gives both 108 × 64 × 69 mm and 4.4 × 2.6 × 2.8 inches; these are not exact equivalents. The larger converted dimensions are 111.76 × 66.04 × 71.12 mm. The existing padded envelope, with the long dimension along robot Y, is 70 X × 114 Y × 76 Z mm. Its protection circuit and PP30 discharge connector remain intact. Use its BPC-1502DC 14.6 V, 2 A LiFePO4 charger. [Bioenno battery and charger specification](https://www.bioennopower.com/products/12v-6ah-lifepo4-battery-pvc)

The defined 7 Ah alternative is Power-Sonic PS-1270 F2. Its current sheet gives 151 × 65 × 94 mm case dimensions and 100 mm total height over the terminals, with ±2 mm dimensional tolerance. The standard battery is approximately 1.97 kg; the sheet lists 2.3 kg for the VDS variant. Capacity is 7 Ah at 0.35 A to 10.5 V, but only 4.27 Ah at the stated one-hour rate. The sheet recommends PSC-12500ACX. Its published short-circuit current is 196 A. Use the F2 version when buying a new pack. An existing F1 version needs matching F1 contacts. [PS-1270 datasheet, version 1.2, 2026-08-18](https://www.power-sonic.com/wp-content/uploads/datasheets/ps-1270.pdf)

The defined 14 Ah alternative is Power-Sonic PS-12140 F2. The current sheet gives 151 × 98 × 95 mm case dimensions and 101 mm total height, with ±2 mm dimensional tolerance. Its approximate mass is 3.78 kg. Capacity is 14 Ah at 0.7 A to 10.5 V, and 8.5 Ah at the stated one-hour rate. The recommended charger is PSC-121000ACX. Its published short-circuit current is 392 A. [PS-12140 datasheet, version 1.2, 2026-08-18](https://www.power-sonic.com/wp-content/uploads/datasheets/ps-12140.pdf)

An older official summary lists the PS-12140 at 4.2 kg and 100 mm total height. Use the newer, taller dimensions for the CAD and allow the older, heavier battery in the load assessment. Record the label, measured dimensions and mass of the actual purchased unit. A 14 Ah label alone does not establish fit. [Power-Sonic earlier summary](https://cdn.power-sonic.com/documents/PS-Summary-Specification-Sheet-2.pdf)

These are AGM valve-regulated sealed lead-acid batteries. The design does not cover flooded motorcycle batteries, two batteries connected together, or every battery sold as a standard 7 Ah or 14 Ah unit. Only one battery is connected at a time.

## CAD envelope and access requirements

Use robot X for width and Y for front-to-rear length. Rotate both SLA packs so their 151 mm dimension follows Y. The combined maximum bare-battery envelope is 100 X × 153 Y × 103 Z mm. The 103 mm height already includes terminal blades and dimensional tolerance. It excludes attached contacts, boots and bent wire.

The proposed clear cradle is 104 X × 157 Y mm. This admits the maximum case with 2 mm foam on each side and each end. Foam pieces stay on the cradle. Fill unused space around the smaller 7 Ah or Bioenno pack with firm nonconductive pads. The pads must prevent lateral movement without pressing on terminals, seams or valves. The platform legs carry the boards directly to the base. The battery does not support the platform.

With the base floor at local Z6 and a 2 mm bottom pad, the largest terminal envelope ends at Z111. The platform underside atZ140 leaves29mm above the bare terminal envelope before PCB hardware is fitted. PCB screws, nuts and washers must project no more than3mm below the deck, leaving at least26mm above the bare terminal envelope. Keep attached battery contacts, boots, tie heads and wire bends belowZ130, giving at least7mm clearance to the permitted PCB hardware. These are geometric allowances, not a verified connector fit. Change the connector routing if the actual hardware fails that check; do not raise or modify the printed platform without new fit checks.

The existing skirt's top opening is 184 mm. A 100 ×153 mm rectangle has a 182.7813 mm diagonal. That leaves only 0.6094 mm radial clearance when centered. Paint, print error, tilted insertion and terminal boots can remove that margin. The supported service procedure must therefore allow the whole skirt to lift off the base. Remove the printed platform before removing the battery. Passing the battery through the top is an optional measured fit, with foam left behind and contacts fully within the outline. Do not force the pack through the opening.

The new platform's PCB supports must keep board undersides and solder joints clear of the deck. Provide ventilation below the regulators and access for both battery terminals. Zip ties can retain boards only across suitable bare board edges or insulated saddles. They must not cross hot inductors, connectors, soldered leads or components. The regulator mounting holes remain M2; do not enlarge them to accept M2.5 hardware. [Pololu regulator outline and mounting holes](https://www.pololu.com/product/4091)

## Battery retention

Provide at least two independent transverse tie paths at separate Y positions. Use padded contact with the battery case and rounded printed slot edges. Locate the paths away from terminal blades, vents and cables. The loops pull the battery toward the base; separate pads and stops resist sliding. Do not make the board platform the battery restraint.

Allow at least 500 mm tie length. The bare maximum transverse path is already approximately 2 × (100 +103) =406 mm before the floor path, pads and threading tail. A 350 mm strap from the earlier BOM is too short. A design minimum is a 4.8 mm-wide nylon tie with a published loop strength of at least 222 N; slot width must match the chosen product. A larger product is acceptable if its strap and head clear the CAD.

A concrete size/rating reference is KSS CV-500W:500mm long,4.8mm wide and222N minimum loop strength. Its published bundle limit is147mm. The manufacturer page does not dimension ribbon thickness or latch size, so measure the actual tie before accepting it. The digital route uses a1.2mm-thick ribbon; thicker products need a successful full-path hand fit and must not force or scrape a printed slot. The rating belongs to the tie, not the printed slots or complete restraint. Do not join short ties or substitute a7.6mm strap into the6.2mm slots. Replace ties removed during service. [KSS CV-500W manufacturer specification](https://www.kss.com.tw/index_en.php?action=products4&cid=17&fid=3&id=8461&sid=14)

For the soft Bioenno pouch, use broad nonconductive padding beneath each tie. Tighten only enough to prevent movement. Do not dent or crease its wrap. The base proof load and restraint check must use the actual installed battery mass. No tie or printed-slot load rating has been verified here.

## Adapter, fuses and charging

Keep the existing keyed PP30 robot input. Make one labeled SLA adapter from the selected terminal type to the same PP30 polarity. F2 is a 6.35 mm-wide, 0.8 mm-thick blade; F1 is 4.75 mm wide and 0.8 mm thick. Use matching insulated female quick-disconnects rated for at least 10 A DC and the selected 18 AWG copper wire. Cover each terminal independently. Measure positive polarity at the assembled PP30 before connecting it. Do not leave alligator clips inside the robot. [Power-Sonic terminal definitions](https://www.power-sonic.com/wp-content/uploads/datasheets/ps-12120-12121-2.pdf)

Locate the single existing 7.5 A main fuse F1 within 100 mm of the SLA positive terminal. This maximum includes the short adapter, PP30 connection and unfused lead to F1. F1 stays after the PP30 interface in the existing connection schedule. Fully insulate and restrain the short unfused path. For the Bioenno pack, place F1 within 100 mm of its intact factory PP30. Do not add a second main fuse. Keep the 3 A motor-converter, 3 A servo-converter and 1 A logic-converter branch fuses. The larger battery does not justify larger fuses or motor-current limits. These are project harness requirements based on the existing 50 W allowance in `electronics/calculations.json`.

The Power-Sonic PSC-121000ACX is an automatic 12 V, 1 A SLA charger with a stated minimum battery capacity of 3 Ah. It is the recommended 14 Ah charger and its capacity range also admits 7 Ah. The 7 Ah battery's own suggested PSC-12500ACX is another supported choice. Use the exact charger instructions and terminal polarity; charge with the battery removed and disconnected from the robot. The current charger page has inconsistent generic voltage fields, so this design does not turn those fields into a homemade charging profile. [PSC-121000ACX charger specification](https://www.power-sonic.com/product/psc-121000acx/?preview=true), [PSC-12500ACX charger](https://www.power-sonic.com/product/psc-12500acx/?preview=true)

Keep the LiFePO4 and SLA chargers separately labeled. Never use the TTGO battery socket as a charge connection for either 12 V pack. No battery is charged inside the body. Keep ventilation paths open during operation; sealed lead-acid does not mean gas-tight. The manufacturer prohibits use in an airtight enclosure and calls for load disconnection at the end of discharge. Recharge after use rather than storing a discharged SLA pack. [Power-Sonic technical manual, pages 12 and 21](https://cdn.power-sonic.com/documents/SLA_Technical_Manual.pdf)

## Existing power and firmware compatibility

The two Pololu D36V50F5 regulators accept 5.5–50 V and retain their 5 V outputs. The MPM3610 board accepts 4.5–21 V and also stays at 5 V. Thus the selected SLA batteries do not need a different regulator voltage. Actual current capacity still depends on heat and airflow. Preserve the existing regulator temperature and rail-voltage tests after moving the boards. [Pololu 4091](https://www.pololu.com/product/4091), [Adafruit MPM3610](https://www.adafruit.com/product/4739)

The existing 100k/22k divider produces 2.6508 V at a 14.7 V input. With the top resistor 1% low and the bottom 1% high, the result is 2.6946 V. The existing firmware allows plausible readings up to 15.0 V. No charging source is connected while operating. The divider and this operating range therefore remain suitable for the selected batteries. These values are calculated, not ADC measurements.

`firmware/include/config.h` currently stops motion at 11.2 V and permits re-arm at 12.0 V. Keep these common limits; they stop before the selected batteries' stated 10.5 V low-rate capacity endpoint. This does not provide a charge percentage or guarantee a particular depth of discharge. The current firmware disables motion but keeps the controller and audio supply powered. An SLA pack has no Bioenno-style internal protection. After any low-battery stop, switch MAIN off and unplug the battery. Leave it unplugged between uses. Do not claim that the current firmware gives an unattended full-battery disconnect.

## Finished mass and drive limits

The final FACET-1 slice report predicts2.47398kg of installed printed plastic for the motor-clamp option. It excludes support waste and purchased hardware. Five30.6g motors, five38g wheels and four14g servos add0.399kg, for a2.87298kg partial subtotal. These terms are also recorded in `electronics/calculations.json`. [Required motor mass and torque](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3777), [required wheel mass](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3766), [servo mass](https://www.adafruit.com/product/2307)

Adding the battery gives incomplete robot totals of3.57298kg with Bioenno,4.84298kg with PS-1270, and6.65298kg with the current PS-12140. The older4.2kg battery makes that partial total7.07298kg. An explicit1kg allowance for all remaining electronics, bearings, fasteners, wiring, pads and finish gives roughly4.57,5.84 and7.65kg respectively, or8.07kg with the older heavy pack. The allowance is not a measured BOM sum or a payload rating. Weigh the finished robot.

The existing calculation estimates6.245N of combined ground-wheel force at the driver current limits, using a linear current/torque approximation. It is not a continuous rating. At the illustrative7.65kg mass, the force is only about0.0832 times robot weight; steering scrub uses part of that force. Neither motor nor wheel listing supplies a robot payload or allowable gearbox shaft load. SLA support establishes a defined fit and electrical option. Loaded driving remains a physical acceptance gate.

Test the complete mass on hard, level indoor flooring with slow starts and broad moving turns. Retain the existing 5 V supply and 1 A bridge limits. Reject a build that binds, repeatedly stops, drops a rail below 4.8 V, overheats, or deforms its printed supports. Do not increase motor voltage or defeat current limiting to move the heavier battery. Use the retained Bioenno pack if the SLA build fails the loaded tests. The requested drive parts stay unchanged.
