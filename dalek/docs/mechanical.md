# ROUND-10 mechanical design and assembly

The revised robot has a circular 300 mm base, four concealed ground wheels, complete vertically stacked body sections, concealed arm servos, and an adjustable TT-motor friction drive for the head. There are ten STL designs and eleven printed pieces: print `07_pitch_carrier.stl` twice. Each STL is one connected part. Nominal assembled height is 563.8 mm, below the requested 914.4 mm limit.

The reference review covers 100 distinct photographs. The resulting details include a dark round fender, four rows of hemispheres with raised bezels, twelve aligned skirt seams, shoulder slats and rivets, rounded gunboxes and spherical arm roots, three neck rings over a dark liner, eyestalk discs, a stepped lens, tilted ribbed lamps, dome seams, telescoping arm bands and an eight-rod decorative emitter. The circular footprint and hidden motor drive are custom adaptations. See `appearance-research.md` and `reference-image-review.csv`.

This is a digitally checked first-prototype design. No print strength, motor payload, tire grip, runtime, actual bearing fit or assembled robot motion has been physically measured. Mesh checks, component-envelope sampling and real H2D slicing establish the limited digital results described in `revision-review.md` and the generated validation reports. Complete the physical gates below before operation.

## Print inventory

Use the released STL files, a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layers and single-nozzle H2D mode. The single-nozzle envelope is 325 x 320 x 325 mm. The circular base plus its requested 8 mm brim is 316 x 316 mm. The other parts request 5 mm brims. Keep the print on the bed. The actual slicing report is authoritative for generated supports, brim paths and the exact released STL hashes. The checked process uses normal automatic supports. Do not substitute a different orientation or auto-arrangement without checking its complete toolpath envelope. [Bambu H2D specification](https://cdn1.bambulab.com/documentation/h2d/en/H2D_Laser_Full_Combo_20250305.pdf).

1. `01_base.stl`: PETG, floor down. Six walls, six top/bottom layers and 40% gyroid. Remove supports through the bottom wheel openings. The body is diameter 300 x 54 mm, plus a 3 mm registry tongue. Its floor is 6 mm thick. Main ribs are 4 mm thick. The outside bumper remains continuous around the full circle.
2. `02_lower_skirt.stl`: PLA, large opening down. Four walls and 15% infill. A complete circular section changes from diameter 300 to 250 over 110 mm, plus its tongue. Shallow seam relief slightly increases the bounding box.
3. `03_upper_skirt.stl`: PLA, large opening down. Four walls and 15% infill. Diameter 250 to 220 over 100 mm, plus its tongue.
4. `04_shoulder.stl`: PETG, bottom flange down. Four walls and 25% infill. Its body is 120 mm high, plus its tongue. Both concealed gunboxes, servo pockets, speaker baffle and rear display frame are integral. Remove support from inside each gunbox before installing hardware.
5. `05_neck.stl`: PETG, bottom flange down. Four walls and 30% infill. Rings, opaque grille liner, bearing tower, slide deck, adjustment lug, hard stop and service ports are integral. Clear the bearing seats, lower shaft slot and all tool channels.
6. `06_head.stl`: PLA, drum rim down. Four walls and 15% infill. The friction drum, hub, spokes, dome, eye and lamps are one part. Remove internal support through the drum and crown openings. Keep the inside drum surface smooth and free of paint, glue and support scars.
7. `07_pitch_carrier.stl`: two identical PETG parts, floor down. Four walls and 30% infill. Each carrier is 49 x 30 x 49 mm. Its Y bounds are -10 to +20 mm, not symmetric about the yaw shaft.
8. `08_plunger_arm.stl`: PLA, as exported and placed on the bed. Four walls and 15% infill. The spherical cover, stem bands and cup are integral.
9. `09_emitter_arm.stl`: PLA, as exported and placed on the bed. Four walls and 15% infill. The spherical cover, eight rods and muzzle are integral. This is decorative and has no projectile mechanism.
10. `10_head_motor_carriage.stl`: PETG, floor down. Four walls and 30% infill. Its radial slots and padded motor saddle form one part. This replaces the old head-servo pulley file; there is no eleventh STL.

Paint the bumper, neck liner, outside of the head drum, socket roots and plunger cup near-black. Use bronze body panels, gold relief, silver stems and pale eye discs. Paint the outside only; keep all bearing seats, sliding faces, horn seats and the friction track clean. Read actual mesh dimensions and material-volume bounds from `bom/printed-parts.csv`. Read predicted deposited mass and support consumption from the current H2D slice report. Neither number is a verified assembled mass.

## Coordinate map and stack joints

Dimensions are millimetres. Positive Y is rear, positive X is right and Z points up. World Z0 is the floor. The nominal base datum is world Z13.8: wheel radius 31.5 minus floor thickness 6 minus the drawing-derived 11.7 mm case-bottom-to-axle datum. Actual motor padding and axle height must be measured.

- Base origin: world Z13.8; body top 67.8.
- Lower skirt origin: 67.8; body top 177.8.
- Upper skirt origin: 177.8; body top 277.8.
- Shoulder origin: 277.8; body top 397.8.
- Neck origin: 397.8. Lower bearing is local Z40-47; upper bearing is Z59-66.
- Head STL origin: world Z449.8, or neck-local Z52. Its drum starts at print Z0. Its hub foot is print Z15, world Z464.8. Dome rim is world Z488.8. Lamp tops are approximately 563.8.
- Ground-wheel axes: X +/-98, Y +/-58, world Z31.5. The fully seated hub accommodation range is abs(X) 95.5-100.5, not an instruction to stop a hub short.
- Arm yaw axes: shoulder-local (-47,-94,42) and (53,-94,42). Each pitch-axis offset in the yaw frame is (-3,0,35). Neutral spherical centers are world (-50,-94,354.8) and (50,-94,354.8).
- Head carriage origin at nominal contact: neck-local (64.5,0,29.2). Head wheel center: (64.5,0,71); its shaft is vertical and its tire occupies local Z56.5-85.5.

Four M4x20 screws retain each of the four horizontal body joints. Hole radii are 137 mm for base/lower, 112 for lower/upper and 97 for the two upper joints. All four holes are at 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees. Flanges are 18 mm wide and 6 mm thick. The tongue is 2 mm wide and 3 mm high; its groove provides nominal 0.3 mm clearance per side. Align the hemisphere columns and the front seam before tightening. The tongue locates the joint; bolts retain it.

Use a 150 mm ball-end 3 mm driver rated for 20 degrees for the lower stack joints, plus a 7 mm spanner for M4 nuts. The lower-ring bottom bolts require an approximately 18.4-degree inward approach. The neck provides four 9.2 mm vertical tool channels, so its bolts can be tightened with a straight driver before fitting the head.

The motor drawing identifies an 18.6 mm gearbox, 22.4 mm metal-end width, 22.44 mm case height, 70 mm length and 36.6 mm across shaft ends. The product page's simplified body dimensions differ from that drawing; the CAD uses the drawing envelopes. Measure the real shaft, fully seated hub, case and leads. The arm motors are four MG92B servos. Adafruit specifies a 36 x 12 x 31 mm overall envelope including mounting features. The CAD provides measured-fit padded mounts; the modeled 24 mm case length is an assembly envelope, not a claimed manufacturer case drawing. Check the actual body, mounting ears, spline location and supplied horn. Use the supplied spline horns and center screws. Do not print a replacement spline. [MG92B](https://www.adafruit.com/product/2307).

The original TTGO T-Display PCB is nominally 51.52 x 25.04 mm. The rear recess is 52.2 x 26 mm. Insert the confirmed original 1.14-inch ESP32 board from inside, behind the lip. Screen, buttons and USB remain accessible. The S3 and AMOLED boards are different. [Original board files](https://github.com/Xinyuan-LilyGO/TTGO-T-Display).

The Adafruit 1313 speaker is 77.8 x 77.8 x 25.49 mm with 60 mm mounting centers. Its new internal baffle is at Y-20 to -16, center shoulder-local Z43. The speaker body extends rearward. The battery is the specified Bioenno BLF-1206A 12 V, 6 Ah pack. It is rotated in plan to 70 mm X by 114 mm Y, with nominal height 76 mm. Use the matching charger listed in the electrical BOM. ## Build the circular motor base

1. Clear supports, shaft ports, clamp holes, wiring exits and strap slots. Inspect floor and ribs for voids, warping or cracks. Confirm all four motor floors are coplanar. Do not scale the whole part to correct one hole.
2. Press each real wheel fully onto its motor outside the base. Motor centerlines are X +/-70.5, with axles at Y +/-58. Motor tails point toward Y0 and stop nominally at Y +/-1. Leads leave upward through the central openings. Do not put wire slack between the motor ends.
3. Lower the four motor/wheel pairs from above. Cases sit on the 6 mm floor. Add measured side pads for the narrower gearbox. Under-case pads change axle height and must be applied consistently if required. Both shaft ends must turn freely.
4. Use two stainless strips per motor, each 43 x 8 x 1 mm, with 3.4 mm holes 37 mm apart. Deburr and place nonconductive padding under each strip. The mirrored clamp axes are abs(X) 51.5 and 88.5 at abs(Y) 6 and 16. The bosses end at local Z29. Adjust padding so the strip lies flat without crushing the case.
5. Install sixteen M3x40 screws, washers and nuts. The full motor floor carries the cases; side saddles resist lateral motion and metal strips provide retention. Check screw tips retain ground clearance. Turn every wheel completely and inspect the hub, tire runout and both axle ends.
6. The wheel opening is limited to radius 147, retaining a continuous 3 mm outer bumper at its closest location. Its inboard boundary is abs(X) 80.5 and its roof ends at local Z52. The nominal tire top is local Z49.2. At the most inward accommodated hub, the tire's inner face is X81. The electronics plate ends at X80; that 1 mm nominal gap needs a real loaded-runout check. Never leave a wheel partly seated to obtain it.
7. Keep all motor leads, suppression capacitors and strain relief above the well and away from the rotating tire. Reject any rubbing before proceeding.

## Battery, raised plates and lower stack

Bolt the lower skirt onto the bare base before installing the electronics plates. Fit the nuts below the upper base flange and use the angled driver through the lower skirt. Seat the tongue completely before tightening.

The battery pocket has 76 x 116 mm clear plan space and a continuous 6 mm floor. Rotate the 70 x 114 mm pack to match it. Use two independent 20 mm straps through the slots at X +/-34, Y +/-35. Pad and restrain the pack without crushing it. The left connector notch is X-46 to -36, Y-22 to 22. Route its lead upward in the fixed side gap; secure the lead before it can reach a motor tail.

Cut two unclad FR4 plates to 160 x 56 x 2 mm. Round and deburr the edges. Their centers are (0,-93) and (0,93), so X is -80 to 80 and Y is -121 to -65 or 65 to 121. Eight plate mounts are X +/-54 at Y -93,-69,69,93. Use 25 mm nylon spacers on the 9 mm-high pads, placing plate undersides at base-local Z34. Fit M2.5x40 fasteners and check two complete threads beyond each nut.

Use each actual PCB as a drilling template on the FR4; do not drill or assume a universal PCB pattern. The two 81 x 51 mm protoboards lie with their 81 mm dimension along X, centered at X-35, Y +/-93. Use 6 mm PCB spacers and the M2.5 board hardware. Component positions and connector clearance are specified in the electrical chapter. The third DRV8833 drives the head. It fits on the existing rear plate; there is no extra printed electronics mount.

Install the upper skirt next. Test disconnectable harness loops before closing the shoulder joint. Electronics plates remain separately removable. Remove them before accessing the motor clamps or base-joint nuts.

## Concealed arm mechanisms and rear board

Assemble the shoulder on the bench with its top opening accessible. Both MG92B pairs and their horns belong inside the rounded gunboxes. Only spherical roots and clean stems emerge from the sockets. The final software limit is 8 degrees on each arm axis; do not restore the former 12-degree limit.

1. Center the four servos electrically before attaching their supplied horns. Keep the arms detached. The yaw support face is shoulder-local Z8 and the target horn face is Z42. Select pads from actual measurements. Thread two cable ties through each yaw pocket before installing the servo; tighten without crushing the case.
2. Keep both pitch-servo carriers outside the shoulder while feeding the arms. Feed each detached arm tip from inside through the 44 mm front socket. The 40 mm plunger cup is the limiting part. Check the actual painted cup with a caliper. The spherical cap stays inside; it does not pass through the smaller front opening. An installed pitch servo blocks this feed path.
3. A nominal insertion path tilts the detached arm 20 degrees downward and keeps its axis through socket center Y-115, Z77. With X at +50 or -50, move the arm pivot from Y+30 to -20 while Z = 77 + (Y + 115) * tan(20 degrees). Keep Y-20 while reducing tilt from 20 to zero and keep Z = 77 + 95 * tan(tilt). Then advance the horizontal arm pivot from Y-20 to -94 at Z77. Keep the stem centered in the opening. Support both arms in this final horizontal position with padded blocks under the exposed stems. Their root centers are shoulder-local (-50,-94,77) and (50,-94,77). Keep the blocks outside the body and leave the interior clear. Do not let the unsupported arms load or scrape the socket edges.
4. Assemble each pitch-servo carrier outside the shoulder. The carrier's yaw-horn attachment is its floor. Attach the pitch servo to the carrier's side wall with its supplied horn facing the pitch axis. Use the two tie locations and a measured pad. Relative to the yaw origin, the checked pitch-case envelope is X4..31,Y-6..6,Z13..49. The pitch axis is (-3,0,35). Keep the lead loop behind the carrier, with slack for both axes.
5. Keep both arms supported while inserting each carrier and pitch servo. Pass the assembly through the central top opening at approximately X+29 or -29, Y-44. The right carrier's extreme plan corner is (64,-54), radius 83.74, inside the 92 mm opening. Lower the carrier floor to Z46. First move it sideways to its final yaw X53 or -47 while keeping Y-44. Then move it forward to Y-94 and lower it to Z42. Seat it on the centered yaw horn and install the supplied horn-center screw and the two M2 fasteners. Do not force the assembled gimbal straight down at its final forward position. This path clears the supported arms in the nominal geometry.
6. Attach each supported arm plate to its supplied pitch horn using two M2x10 screws, washers and nuts, plus the horn's supplied center screw. Remove its support only after the horn and fasteners retain the arm. Both arm STLs include a 28 mm print-coordinate offset. The assembly transform removes that offset, rotates 90 degrees about X and -90 degrees about Z, then applies pitch and parent yaw.
7. Fit an opaque flexible socket liner to each gunbox so no motor, horn, tie or lead is visible around the moving root. Cut a 44 mm central opening in the listed black stretch fabric. Use a radial slit with an overlapped seam. Bond the inner edge inside the rear rim of the spherical cap and the outer edge around the fixed socket. Retain loose folds between these attachment edges. Keep adhesive on fixed surfaces and the intended moving attachment only. Do not allow fabric to enter a horn, bind an axis, contact the speaker cone or pull a lead. The opaque liner is purchased fabric, not an extra STL. Inspect from the front, both sides and below at all four yaw/pitch extremes.
8. Install the speaker on its internal baffle and put acoustic cloth behind the front grille. Insert the T-Display from inside the rear frame. Use four padded retaining tabs, M3x25 screws, washers and nuts. Touch only bare PCB edges with the pads; keep components, antenna, display glass, USB and both buttons clear. Fit the two rear switches and service plugs.

Run twenty slow circles at a small amplitude before increasing to 8 degrees. Reject buzzing, wire tension, fabric snagging or a visible actuator. The spherical cap has radius 28 with a 2.5 mm wall, while the fixed spherical cavity is radius 29.2. These are nominal CAD clearances, not permission to force an oversized painted print through the socket.

## Bearing stack and adjustable friction drive

The head load travels through the hub, steel spindle, two 608 bearings, metal inner-race spacer and neck structure. The tire supplies tangential drive and adjustable contact; it does not support the head's weight. There is no head servo, belt or servo pulley.

1. With the neck still on the bench, clean both 22.2 mm bearing seats. Fit the lower 608 bearing at neck-local Z40-47 and upper bearing at 59-66. Put the 12 mm-long, 8.1 mm-ID, 12 mm-OD metal spacer between their inner races. Fit the narrow 8 x 12 x 1 mm shims below and above the inner-race stack. Retain outer races with three M3 plastic thread-forming screws and 9 mm washers at each end. Do this before fastening the neck onto the shoulder; its lower retainer is not inserted through a service port.
2. Insert the M8x70 spindle from below. It must pass freely through both 8 mm bearing bores. Keep the narrow shims on the inner races and verify free rotation. Do not pull a misaligned bearing into place with the spindle nut.
3. Lower the neck onto the shoulder, connect the fixed harness and tighten its four M4 joints through the vertical tool channels. Leave the head off.
4. Fit the fifth TT motor on its side in the head carriage. Its axle is vertical; the metal tail points toward negative Y. Use measured thin pads and two ties through the saddle. The nominal motor transform from the original drawing frame (x,y,z) is neck-local (64.5+z-11.7,y,43.5+x). The metal-end bottom is Z32.3 and the carriage floor top is 32.2. Support the narrower gearbox with measured padding rather than forcing it down. The unused lower shaft has a 15 mm-wide radial clearance slot through both carriage and fixed deck.
5. Fit its wheel fully. Nominal wheel center is neck-local (64.5,0,71). Measure actual seating; use appropriate case-height shims to keep the 29 mm tire within Z56.5-85.5 and clear of the head spokes at Z91. Both shaft ends and the hub must remain free. Keep motor wires fixed below the rotating drum.
6. Install the carriage on the fixed deck using four M3x16 screws. Fixed screw axes are (43,-50),(43,8),(83.5,-40),(83.5,8). Use 6 mm-OD, 1 mm-thick washers above the carriage and standard washers and M3 nyloc nuts below the deck. The small top washer diameter is required for the full slide travel. Leave the screws just loose enough to slide; retain all nuts. The deck is Z25.2-29.2; carriage floor is 29.2-32.2.
7. Insert one M3 nut into the open-top adjustment-lug pocket. Fit the M3x25 jackscrew along positive X through Y-35, Z38. The screw tip bears on the carriage pad. Start with carriage X61.5, nominally 3 mm clear of the inside track. Radial slots permit movement to X65.1; the fixed stop at X90.1 limits the outward plate edge. Do not enlarge slots or bypass that stop to cure a binding head.
8. Lower the head over the spindle with the tire released. The drum is 192 mm inside diameter and 198 mm outside diameter. Its bottom is neck-local Z52 and the spokes start at Z91. Fit the M8 washer above the printed hub and the nyloc nut using a 13 mm socket no more than 23 mm outside diameter through the 24 mm crown hole. Set retention while keeping free bearing rotation. Do not tighten until the head binds.
9. Turn the dome by hand through two complete revolutions while the tire remains released. Check drum roundness, axial runout, crown retention, eye sweep and clearance from the stationary liner. The liner ends at Z51, giving nominal 1 mm clearance below the rotating drum.
10. With power off and the head seated, reach the jackscrew from the left through its service port at Y-40..-30,Z34.5..41.5. Advance it only until the tire makes light, reliable contact. Nominal geometric contact is X64.5. The final 0.6 mm travel is a geometric allowance, not a measured force setting or a required amount of compression. Excess pressure loads the motor shaft and head bearings.
11. Hold the adjustment while tightening all four slide clamps against their M3 nyloc nuts. These clamps lock the setting; there is no separate jackscrew jam nut. The clamp screws remain accessible with a 2.5 mm L-key whose short leg is 15 mm. Its horizontal shaft runs near neck-local Z49.9 through the 3.8 mm-high ports; the short leg reaches the screw socket near Z34.9. Approach the two negative-Y screws from the front and the two positive-Y screws from the rear at their specified X coordinates. Use a correctly sized tool; do not pry open a printed port.
12. Recheck two full hand turns after locking. Start powered tests at low duty in each direction. Increase contact only enough to avoid unwanted slip, then lock and repeat. Do not increase pressure to overcome an eccentric drum, tight spindle nut or snagged liner. Recheck after the motor and PETG mount warm during the short commissioning run.

The head controller commands speed and direction; it has no measured angle feedback. The rolling ratio is approximately 96/31.5=3.05 motor-wheel turns per head turn when there is no slip. Actual slip, speed and stopping distance must be measured. The eye and dome lights are decorative and contain no moving wires, so no slip ring is required.

## Physical acceptance and service

- Before motor installation, support all four motor-floor regions on equal rigid blocks. Apply twice the intended complete robot mass across the battery and stack load points for ten minutes, close to the floor. Unload and inspect for cracks or permanent distortion. This is a proposed proof test, not a verified rating.
- Verify fully seated wheels and free rotation unloaded and under the intended mass. Check the minimum plate/tire gap, bumper clearance and ground clearance with actual tire deformation.
- Weigh the complete robot. Four wide tires resist tight turns. Start on a level hard floor with slow starts and broad moving arcs. Measure current and temperatures. Do not assume these small TT motors can pivot in place, climb thresholds or run on carpet because the base is reinforced.
- Run twenty arm circles while inspecting every socket from low, side and frontal angles. The fabric must remain opaque, loose and clear of moving hardware at all permitted poses.
- Run a full head turn in each direction, check runout and repeat all contact/lock checks warm. A slipping drive is preferable to forcing a bound bearing with excessive pressure.
- Complete the electrical stop, Wi-Fi-loss, power-switch and simultaneous-load tests in `assembly.md`. The physical actuator switch must remain reachable.

For service, isolate power, unplug labelled harnesses and remove the required horizontal joint screws. Lift whole body sections vertically. Remove the neck and disconnect the four arm connectors to service the shoulder; fit the internal mechanisms while its top is open. The motor plates can be removed separately for base service. Back off the head jackscrew before lifting the dome. Remove its crown nut with the specified socket and lift by the dome rim, never by the eye or lamps. Refit socket liners and repeat the motion checks after service.
