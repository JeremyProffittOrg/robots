# R2-D2 image review — body, skirt and full-robot views

Category: front body panels, rear body panels, skirt, body-to-dome ring, full-robot views in
two-leg and three-leg stance, and proportions of dome, body, legs and feet.

Method. Every file below was downloaded with a script to `research/images/body-NN.jpg`,
checked to be larger than 15 kB, opened with Pillow, and then opened and looked at with the
Read tool. Each entry describes only what is visible in that file. All pixel measurements
are estimates made by eye on the downloaded file; they are not read from a drawing. The
part names follow the printed-droid.com terminology page
(https://www.printed-droid.com/kb/r2-d2-terminology/), which was fetched and quoted for
entries 17 to 20. Twenty images were reviewed; the task asked for at least 11.

Files not kept. Six candidates were downloaded, viewed, and deleted because they were not
useful for this category: a steampunk wooden R2 (Steam Punks at The Nothe Fort), a group
photo where R2 is tiny (C2E2 2015), a LEGO brick R2 (BC 20141004-0756), a Sphero toy display
(NYCC 2017 08), a model-kit R2 on a Star Destroyer hull (Sully VA panoramio), and five human
cosplay dresses from Category:Cosplay of R2-D2.

## Key findings for CAD

1. Front panel layout, top to bottom, is fixed and was consistent on every droid viewed
   (entries 1-5, 10-13, 17): large data port slot just under the dome ring; two utility
   arm pockets; two stacked front vents in the centre; six coin slots left of the vents;
   bottom row of pocket vent, coin return, power coupling (centre), octagon port (right);
   skirt. Model these as cutouts and pockets in the front ring STLs, not as separate
   glued greeblies, so the "fewest STLs" rule is kept.
2. Rear panel layout (entry 19): one large rectangular rear door in the upper two thirds,
   then a bottom row of pocket vent, coin return, power coupling, coin return, octagon
   port. Use the rear door as the battery and electronics access door; make it a hinged
   or magnet-held printed panel at the same size ratio (about 0.6 body width by 0.5 body
   height, estimated).
3. Side body doors (entry 20) hinge on their outer vertical edge and need a stiff frame
   ring behind them. In the stacked-ring body, put the door openings entirely inside one
   ring so the hinge bosses are printed into that ring.
4. Dome-to-body interface (entries 5, 7, 8, 9): the body ends in a flat top ring plate with
   a raised locating ring; the dome ring band is about 0.06 body diameter tall and has the
   same outer diameter as the body. Put the lazy-susan bearing and the friction-drive
   wheel inside this ring; the top plate needs a large central hole for the slip ring
   loom.
5. Skirt (entries 14, 16, 17): a separate ribbed band under the body that slopes inward
   about 20 to 25 degrees (estimated) and is about 0.1 body diameter tall; it has the
   centre-foot opening. Print the skirt as its own ring with the centre leg mount built
   in; the vertical ribs run on the front and rear faces only.
6. Shoulder pivot position (entries 9, 18): on the outer body surface, about 0.3 of body
   height below the top ring, on the body centre line in side view. The shoulder hub cap
   on the leg is about 0.22 body diameter and shows two bolt heads; the body-side shoulder
   disc is about 0.55 body height in diameter. Make the body shoulder bosses part of the
   ring that contains the pivot height, with a steel bolt through to a printed hub.
7. Three-leg stance geometry (entries 1, 4, 10-13, 17-19): body tilts back about 15 to 20
   degrees (estimated) from vertical; outer legs rotate forward at the shoulder so the
   outer foot centres sit about 0.25 body diameter ahead of the body axis; the centre foot
   sits directly under the body axis and about 0.4 body diameter ahead of the outer foot
   centre line; outer feet span about 1.35 to 1.4 body diameters outside to outside. Use
   these to set the index-pin angle at the shoulder and the centre-leg length.
8. Two-leg stance geometry (entries 2, 3): body upright, outer legs vertical, centre foot
   retracted above the skirt line. The centre-foot pocket in the body must be deep enough
   to hide the full foot (about 0.35 body diameter wide, entry 17) when retracted, or the
   design must fix the centre leg down, which is the plan's operating choice.
9. Overall proportions (entry 17, cross-checked with entries 3 and 11): dome height about
   0.47 of body diameter; body height from dome ring to skirt bottom about 1.29 of body
   diameter; total height in three-leg stance about 2.5 body diameters. At the plan's 317 mm
   body this gives about 149 mm dome height, about 409 mm body height, and about 790 mm
   total height (all estimated from pixel ratios; confirm against club drawings in
   `research/proportions.md`).
10. Foot shells (entries 4, 13, 18): about 0.5 body diameter long and 0.25 high, with the
    drive wheels at the rear lower corner of the shell and the ankle pin at the top front.
    The four Adafruit 3766 wheels per foot (63 mm diameter) must fit inside a shell of
    about 158 x 79 mm at this scale, which sets the wheel layout as two axles in line.
11. Battery placement (entry 15): builders put the SLA battery on the lowest shelf at the
    rear of the frame. Match this: the bottom body ring holds the battery bay at the rear,
    behind the centre leg mount, so the battery mass sits low and behind the tilt axis.
12. Every droid viewed shows a wire loop from the foot to the leg (entries 1-4, 11, 17).
    Route the motor wires through the leg channel and out a hole at the ankle; keep a
    printed loop anchor on the foot shell for the visible cable.
