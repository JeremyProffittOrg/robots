# Image review: legs, shoulders, ankles and feet

Category: legs, shoulders, ankles and feet. Twenty images were downloaded to
`research/images/legs-01` to `legs-20`, each was checked with Pillow (real image, unique
MD5, larger than 15 kB), and each was opened and looked at before it was described.

Naming follows the R2 Builders Club terms as listed on the Printed Droid terminology page
(read from https://www.printed-droid.com/kb/r2-d2-terminology/): horseshoe, shoulder hub,
booster covers, leg struts, main leg, shoulder hydraulics, shoulder buttons, ankle
bracelet, outer ankle sides, ankle details, ankle cylinder wedges, ankle cylinders, ankle
cylinder holders, toggle joints (under-shoulder details), main footshell, foot side plate,
half moon details, foot details, battery boxes, battery box harnesses, foot hoses, knurled
fittings, foot stripes, shoulder flanges.

Every angle and size below is marked "estimated" (judged from the picture) or "read"
(quoted from a source with its URL). The design scale is 0.68386 (317 / 463.55, from
`plan.md`), so a club-standard dimension times 0.684 gives the model dimension.

## Key findings for CAD

1. The shoulder is a fixed body-side horseshoe with a central round window and a hub that
   turns inside it. Only the hub and leg rotate. The horseshoe can be a thick printed ring
   bolted to the body; the load goes through the hub bolt into the body frame (images 1, 2,
   4, 5, 11). At model scale, a horseshoe about 0.30 to 0.32 of the body diameter wide
   gives 95 to 101 mm (estimated from images 1 and 19).
2. Make the outer leg one structural plate from the hub seat to the ankle, as in the
   aluminium leg in image 11, and split the print at the ankle bracelet line so the two
   full-length M8 rods locked in `plan.md` carry the bending load across the joint.
3. Fix the two stance angles with index pins: two-leg (leg vertical, body upright) and
   three-leg (leg about 17 to 20 degrees forward from vertical). The builder blog states
   17 degrees (read, http://pauls-second-r2-d2.blogspot.com/2013/07/center-leg-lift-development-overview.html);
   the body tilt seen in images 1, 2, 3, 18, 19 and 20 is estimated at 15 to 19 degrees.
   Use 17 degrees as the design value.
4. The ankle is one horizontal pin through a triangular ankle block (image 10). Add hard
   stops (wedges) so the foot cannot tip past level in either direction; the builder
   caption in image 11 says a free ankle must be secured for 2-3-2 use (read).
5. The foot shell is an open box with no floor. Wheels carry the load, and the shell edge
   floats 10 to 15 mm above the floor at model scale (images 6, 16, 17: estimated 0.05 to
   0.10 of shell height). Keep the shell edge clear of the floor so the foot stripes do not
   drag.
6. Wheel layout used by builders: one tyre plus one caster (image 12) had tipping problems
   on uneven floors (read); two driven wheels in line per foot (image 13) fixed it; a tyre
   plus an omni wheel (image 16) is a printed-droid variant. For this design, put two 3766
   wheels on each of two 3777 motors per foot, one motor pair at the front and one at the
   rear of the shell, so each foot is four-wheel driven and self-stable in pitch.
7. The centre foot is a narrower wedge with no battery box (images 2, 3, 7, 18, 19:
   estimated 0.45 of the body diameter wide, about 0.60 of an outer foot width). Its ankle
   must sit level while the body is tilted 17 degrees; the locked design uses a vertical
   caster pivot on bearings, so the centre foot shell should be symmetrical front to back.
8. The centre leg length sets the stance: the centre foot must be level with the outer feet
   when the body is tilted 17 degrees. Image 18 shows two builds with different centre leg
   lengths and offsets and different stances. Derive the centre leg length from the outer
   leg length, the shoulder height and the tilt, and do not copy a number from a build.
9. The booster cover is a blue trapezoid that starts curved at the hub and fills the leg
   width for about the upper 0.35 of the leg length (images 1, 4, 5). Model it as a raised
   panel on the leg print; no separate part is needed.
10. Leg struts (two exposed cylinders in a channel under the booster cover) and the ankle
    bracelet (a thin ring around the leg at the ankle, image 14 and 15) are cosmetic. Print
    the bracelet flat as one thin part and glue it; print the strut cylinders as part of the
    leg. The hollow between the strut channels is the wire route to the foot (image 15).
11. Foot hoses run from two knurled fittings at the bottom of the battery box, loop on the
    floor, and go up into the ankle (images 3, 6, 20). At model scale use a hose about
    0.06 of the foot length in diameter (about 12 mm) and leave two through holes in the
    ankle side for them.
12. The outer feet are about 1.0 to 1.05 body diameters apart at their outer faces
    (images 2, 18, 20: estimated). At model scale that is 317 to 333 mm, which sets the
    shoulder hub spacing plus twice the leg-to-foot offset.
13. The foot shell length is about 0.60 to 0.62 of the body diameter and its height about
    0.28 (images 1, 19: estimated). At model scale this is about 190 to 197 mm long and
    about 89 mm tall. Two 3777 motors (70 mm long each) with 63 mm wheels fit inside this
    envelope when the motors lie along the foot length with their wheel pairs at each end.
14. The shoulder hub boss on the outside is a stack of concentric rings with a slotted
    centre (images 1, 2, 5). Print the boss as the outer cap of the M8 pivot bolt so the
    bolt head is hidden inside the hub.
